GDP × Industry Weighted All-World

The FTSE All-World GDP Weighted idea, pushed one level down. Country weights follow IMF PPP GDP, as in the FTSE rules. Within each country, weight is split across ICB-based industry buckets in proportion to each bucket's share of market-sector value added in the OECD Inter-Country Input-Output (ICIO) tables. The result is 57 weight sets from 2000-03 to 2027-03: 55 semi-annual reviews plus 2 intra-review deletions.

1. Method

Relation to the FTSE ground rules

FTSE has two GDP-based All-World variants:

  • FTSE GDP Weighted Index Series (v3.8, Jul-2026). Reviewed annually in March. Country weight is proportional to the IMF five-year-ahead forecast of PPP GDP, and stocks are rescaled within each country by free-float market cap (wi = w′i × GDP%j / MC%j), with a 5% company cap.
  • FTSE All-World GDP-Adjusted (v1.0, Jan-2026). Reviewed semi-annually in March and September. It uses IMF nominal USD GDP for the most recent published year: the April WEO feeds the September review and the October WEO feeds the following March review.

This build keeps the FTSE structure but makes the following choices:

Country weightIMF WEO PPP GDP of the latest reported year, not a forecast, as a share of the universe total. The vintage timing follows the GDP-Adjusted rule: March reviews use the October WEO (year Y−2), September reviews use the April WEO (year Y−1).
Industry splitWithin each country, OECD ICIO value added of each bucket ÷ in-scope value added for the latest ICIO year published before the review data cut-off.
Bucket weightwc,b = GDP%c × VA%b|c. Because VA% is a within-country share, ICIO only determines the mix. The more timely IMF figure sets each country's level, which is equivalent to rescaling the lagged ICIO table to IMF GDP.
ScopeMarket sector: ICIO A–N and R–S. O (public administration), P (education), Q (health and social work) and T (households) are excluded. L (real estate) is kept net of owner-occupier imputed rent.
CalendarSemi-annual reviews, implemented after the close on the 3rd Friday of March and September (effective Monday). Data cut-off is the Wednesday before the 1st Friday of the review month.
Intra-review deletionsFollowing rule 6.2, a deleted country's weight is redistributed pro rata across all remaining country × bucket cells. This applies to Morocco (22-Jun-2015) and Russia (07-Mar-2022). There are no intra-review additions.
Stock level (implementation)Map each constituent's ICB subsector to its bucket (icb_subsector_to_bucket.csv). Each country × bucket weight is then spread across its stocks by free-float market cap, the same w′ × target / MC% rescaling as FTSE rule 5.2.2, applied at the country × bucket level. A cell with no constituents needs a redistribution rule, e.g. pro rata within the country (see caveats).

3. Timing and publication lag

IMF. Country weights use the latest reported year, never projections: Y−2 at March reviews (October WEO) and Y−1 at September reviews (April WEO). The two reviews from Mar-2026 and the provisional Mar-2027 use the actual frozen WEO vintages, so they are genuinely point-in-time. Earlier reviews use the year that would have been reported, but with the latest revised values, because the IMF API only serves the last few vintages.

A consequence of this rule is that a September review and the following March review use the same data year. With revised history, country weights therefore only change once a year, except for universe changes. In live use, March picks up the October revision.

ICIO. OECD publishes roughly every two years with a 3-year lag, so at a review the industry mix is 4–7 years old (review year minus data year). The edition timeline below is applied strictly: an edition counts only if it was published before the review's data cut-off. Before the first ICIO release (Jan-2013) the history is simulated with a 4-year lag. Values always come from the 2025 edition, which back-casts 1995–2022 on a consistent basis.

4. Universe: historical FTSE All-World membership

The All-World universe is Developed + Advanced Emerging + Secondary Emerging markets. Membership was reconstructed from FTSE's annual and interim country-classification announcements for 2006–2026. The 2006 classification is assumed to hold from 2000. Phased-in markets enter at the first review after their phase-in completes. The FTSE GDP Weighted rules do this explicitly for Saudi Arabia (eligible from March 2020), and the same rule is applied to Qatar and Kuwait. Countries whose status changed within the All-World universe, such as Korea, Israel, Poland and Greece, are unaffected.

5. ICIO → ICB mapping

Principle: finest common partition

Each of the 173 ICB subsectors is assigned to the ICIO industry that carries its primary economic activity under ISIC Rev.4. A few subsectors genuinely span two ICIO industries according to their ICB definition. Examples are Integrated Oil & Gas (extraction B06 + refining C19), and Iron & Steel, whose ICB definition includes iron-ore mining (B07 + C241). In those cases the two ICIO industries are linked. Buckets are then the connected components of the ICIO ↔ ICB graph. This makes them the finest partition where no ICIO industry and no ICB subsector is ever split: every stock maps to exactly one bucket, and every bucket's GDP measure is a sum of whole ICIO industries.

The result is 39 in-scope buckets. 20 of them span more than one ICB Industry. For example, G (wholesale & retail) holds Consumer Discretionary retailers, Consumer Staples grocers and Industrial distributors; C20 holds chemicals as well as personal and household products. Each bucket carries a judgement-based reporting industry, used only to group charts and never to split weight.

The ICIO tables report the financial sector as a single industry K, so banks and insurers cannot be separated. All 19 financial subsectors, plus payment processors and HMOs, form one bucket.

6. Weight histories

At the 2026-09 review the three largest buckets are Wholesale and retail trade (14.6%), Financials (banks and insurance) (7.7%), Construction (7.6%). China (23.6%), the USA (17.6%) and India (9.9%) dominate on a PPP basis.

The country × bucket matrix is the product of the two inputs. Its rows sum to the country weight; columns are the global bucket weights.

The within-country mix shows where the industry overlay matters most. Agriculture is 20.0% of India's in-scope value added against 1.6% in the USA, which lifts Consumer Staples to 21.7% in India and 19.4% in Indonesia. Energy is 13.0% in Indonesia; Basic Materials is 11.2% in China and 9.9% in Korea; Technology is 12.0% in Korea. Financials are 12.6% in the UK and 10.8% in the USA, against 7.3% in China. Industrials is large everywhere because it collects construction, professional and administrative services.

Turnover is low: on average 1.5% one-way per weight change. The largest changes are at 2019-03-18, 2022-03-07, 2024-03-18, 2026-03-23. These coincide with a new ICIO edition rolling the industry mix forward by 2–4 years, with universe changes (e.g. Kuwait entering in Mar-2019), and with the Russia deletion. Zero turnover means a March review used the same data years as the preceding September, which is inherent to the WEO timing rule with revised history.

Removing owner-occupier imputed rent cuts the global Real Estate bucket from 10.2% to 4.4% at the 2026-09 review. That rent is a national-accounts imputation, with no listed-equity counterpart. The L68A share of L is taken from OECD Table 6A where the split exists and is plausible (0.15–0.97). Shares of ≈1.0, reported by Brazil, Mexico and Saudi Arabia, signal that no split is available. Gaps are interpolated within country; countries without any split (China, India, Japan, Korea, …) use the cross-country median, 61.2% in 2022.

PPP versus market exchange rates is the largest single design lever at the country level. Relative to nominal-USD weights, PPP scales India by 2.7×, Indonesia by 2.1× and China by 1.3×, and the USA by 0.6×. Switching is a one-line change (the NGDPD series is already downloaded).

7. Nuances and caveats

  1. Revised versus real-time data. Only the 2025 ICIO edition and the last three WEO vintages can be downloaded. For earlier reviews the timing is point-in-time (which data year was knowable), but the values are today's revised estimates. Backtests therefore carry mild revision look-ahead, typically small for within-country industry shares.
  2. Pre-2013 ICIO history is simulated. ICIO did not exist before Jan-2013; earlier reviews assume a 4-year lag. Note that the 2018 edition only started in 2005.
  3. Lag mismatch. The industry mix (4–7 years old) lags the country weight (1–2 years old) by 2–6 years. Mixing them is consistent, because shares are scale-free, but structural shifts appear with a delay, e.g. China's construction and real-estate boom and bust, or commodity-price swings in OILG/METL. A possible refinement is to nowcast within-country shares with OECD/UN national-accounts GVA by activity, which has a 1–2 year lag. It is not done here so that the build stays with I/O tables as requested.
  4. PPP versus sector prices. PPP converts whole-economy GDP. Sector price levels differ: non-tradables are relatively cheap in emerging markets. Applying one PPP factor to every bucket is a simplification.
  5. Value added ≠ listed market. Many buckets are dominated by unlisted activity: agriculture, construction, trade, professional services and other personal services. In a stock-level implementation, cells with few or no constituents (e.g. AGRI in the USA, COAL in Japan) need a fallback: redistribute pro rata within the country, pro rata within the bucket across countries, or apply a cap. Concentration also needs controlling, e.g. the 5% company cap from the FTSE rules, applied within cells.
  6. Negative value added. A few ICIO cells are negative (e.g. Brazil C19, 2011–2014, from administered fuel prices). They are summed within buckets and floored at zero.
  7. Missing ICIO countries. Kuwait and Qatar have no ICIO table; Saudi Arabia's industry mix is used as a Gulf proxy. Belgium and Luxembourg are summed, matching FTSE's single Belgium/Luxembourg market.
  8. Thin markets. Romania (Sep-2020) and Iceland (Sep-2022) are Secondary Emerging but may have few or no large/mid-cap All-World constituents. Pakistan exited on 23-Sep-2024. Vietnam's phase-in starts Sep-2026 and is not yet included; edit ftse_allworld_universe.csv to change any of this.
  9. IMF 'latest reported year'. For some countries the September review's Y−1 value is still an IMF staff estimate in the April WEO; these are flagged imf_estimate. Fiscal-year reporters (India, Egypt, Pakistan) follow WEO conventions.
  10. Mapping judgement calls. Software (ISIC 5820 publishing vs 62 programming), packaging, conglomerates and renewable-energy equipment are inherently multi-activity. Each is assigned to its modal activity and documented. Changing a row in icb_to_icio.csv re-derives the buckets automatically, with assertions that no industry is split.
  11. Excluded health care. With Q out of scope, hospitals, labs and other care providers get zero weight. Managed care (HMOs) maps to K, as health insurance. Pharma (C21) and medical devices (C26 / C31T33) stay in.
  12. Provisional review. Mar-2027 is computed from already-published inputs (Oct-2026 WEO, ICIO 2025) and is marked provisional; universe changes announced later are not reflected.
